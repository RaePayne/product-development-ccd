# Corum — Brand Guidelines for AI & Product Development

> **Document purpose:** This file is the single source of truth for any AI system, developer, designer, or copywriter producing work on behalf of Corum. Follow these guidelines precisely. When in doubt, default to clarity, confidence, practicality, and pharmacy-first thinking.

## 1. Brand Identity

### 1.1 Who We Are

**Product name:** Corum
**Company name:** Corum Health Services
**Focus:** Pharmacy software for Australian community pharmacies, built around the realities of LOTS dispensing workflows and day-to-day dispensary operations.

Corum exists to support pharmacists, pharmacy owners, managers, and dispensary staff with the tools they rely on to run a safe, efficient, and compliant pharmacy. Our core product footprint is grounded in the realities of dispensing: prescription queue management, patient lookup, script review, repeat dispensing, stock visibility, and operational oversight.

Pharmacy operations are complex. The right software supports more than transactions — it supports dispensing, customer service, stock control, staff workflow, and business performance. Corum is built for the realities of a busy community pharmacy, where accuracy, speed, compliance, and workflow continuity matter every day.

Corum supports the operational heartbeat of the pharmacy:
- dispensing workflows and script queue processing
- patient and prescription review
- front-of-shop sales and service
- inventory visibility and stock management
- store operations at a single site or across multiple locations

We are not a generic business tool. We are not a broad accounting package. We are pharmacy software designed for the practical realities of Australian community pharmacy and the operational language of LOTS-based dispensing work.

**Tagline (from corumhealth.com.au):** "Where experience and proven platforms meet innovation."

**Positioning (from corumhealth.com.au):** Corum Health is a partner of choice for complete pharmacy software solutions, run independently by pharmacists, focused on helping pharmacy partners "run better businesses."

## 2. Brand Strategy

### 2.1 Mission

To help Australian pharmacies run smarter, safer, and more efficiently through software built around real pharmacy operations.

### 2.2 Purpose

To support the day-to-day needs of community pharmacies — from dispensing and customer service to stock control and multi-store management.

### 2.3 Vision

To be the trusted pharmacy operations platform for Australian community pharmacies, from independent stores to growing multi-location groups.

### 2.4 Brand Promise

Corum helps pharmacy teams manage daily operations with confidence — from the dispensary to the front counter and across the business.

### 2.5 Core Principles

1. **Pharmacy-first design.** Corum is built around real pharmacy workflows, not generic business processes.
2. **Accuracy matters.** Dispensing, stock, and service operations need precision and reliability.
3. **Operational simplicity.** Busy pharmacy teams need software that is practical, usable, and easy to live with every day.
4. **People and workflow come first.** Corum supports pharmacists, managers, dispensary staff, and front-of-shop teams as they work together.
5. **Growth without complexity.** Corum scales from a single store to multi-store pharmacy operations without creating unnecessary operational friction.

Pharmacy management software and pharmacy operations software for Australian community pharmacies and pharmacy groups.

## 6. Voice & Tone

### 6.1 Brand Personality

Corum's brand personality sits at the intersection of trusted and practical. We are:

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Professional | Overly corporate |
| Calm | Flashy or aggressive |
| Practical | Vague or abstract |
| Clear | Jargon-heavy |
| Reliable | Over-promising |
| Business-aware | Detached from operations |
| Human | Cold or impersonal |

### 6.2 Voice Principles

1. **Lead with real outcomes.** Don't describe a feature in isolation. Explain what it does for the pharmacy.
2. **Write for pharmacy teams.** Speak to the people who work in pharmacies every day — owners, pharmacists, managers, dispensary staff, and front-of-shop teams.
3. **Be specific.** "Improve speed and accuracy" is weaker than "reduce prescription processing friction and support a smoother dispensary workflow."
4. **Keep it plain.** Use clear, practical language that business owners and staff understand without decoding software terminology.
5. **Respect operational pressure.** The pharmacy environment is busy. Get to the point. Be clear. Be useful.

### 6.3 Tone Spectrum

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website hero / headline | Direct, outcome-led | "Pharmacy operations, made easier." |
| Product page | Clear, practical, benefit-led | "Support dispensing, retail transactions, and stock control in one system." |
| Case study | Grounded, evidence-based | "A pharmacy team improved workflow visibility and reduced operational friction." |
| Email / nurture | Warm, direct, respectful of time | "Still managing pharmacy operations across disconnected tools?" |
| Social media | Practical, useful, grounded | "A smoother dispensary workflow starts with better visibility." |
| Support / onboarding | Patient, precise, encouraging | "Here's how to get your team set up and working efficiently." |

### 6.4 Signature Phrases & Taglines

These are established directions for use. Use them where appropriate and keep them consistent.

- "Where experience and proven platforms meet innovation." (official tagline)
- "Built for pharmacy operations."
- "Faster, safer, smarter pharmacy workflows."
- "Support the way your pharmacy actually works."
- "From the dispensary to the front counter."
- "One system for daily pharmacy operations."

### 6.5 Words & Phrases We Use

| Use this | Not this |
|---|---|
| Pharmacy | Healthcare software, generic SaaS |
| Dispensing | Prescription processing in an overly generic way |
| Front-of-shop | POS only, unless that is the specific context |
| Stock | Inventory, unless "inventory" is the precise term |
| Pharmacy team | Staff, unless more specific |
| Customer service | Client experience in a generic commercial sense |
| Multi-store | Multi-location, when the context is store operations |
| Pharmacy operations | "Business processes" when pharmacy context is clearer |

### 6.6 Words & Phrases We Avoid

- "Disruptive"
- "Cutting-edge" without meaning
- "Best-in-class"
- "Comprehensive" unless it is genuinely specific
- "Seamless" without explaining what connects
- "Revolutionary"
- "Empower" without saying what changes
- Generic SaaS language that could fit any industry
- Buzzwords that sound like marketing instead of operational value

## 8. Content Guidelines for AI-Generated Work

### 8.1 General Rules

1. Always write from Corum's perspective.
2. Write for pharmacy owners, pharmacists, and operational teams.
3. Lead with operational outcomes.
4. Avoid making up claims, customer names, testimonials, or performance statistics.
5. Default CTA: "Book a demo" or "Request a demo."

### 8.2 SEO & Keyword Guidance

**Primary keywords:**
- pharmacy software Australia
- pharmacy management software
- dispensing software Australia
- pharmacy POS software
- pharmacy inventory software
- pharmacy software for multi-store groups

**Secondary keywords:**
- Corum
- pharmacy dispensing system
- community pharmacy software
- pharmacy stock management software

**Keyword use rules:**
- Integrate naturally
- Write for the reader first
- Match the real language pharmacies use

### 8.3 Content Formats & Templates

**Website product pages:** Start with the problem, explain the pharmacy operations benefit, and end with a clear value statement or CTA.

**Case studies:** Problem → Corum solution → result. Lead with the operational outcome.

**Blog / thought leadership:** 600–1,000 words. One practical insight or operational tip relevant to the pharmacy audience. End with a soft CTA.

**Email subject lines:** Under 50 characters. Outcome or problem-led. No all-caps. No excessive punctuation.

**Social posts (LinkedIn):** 80–150 words. Open with a real pharmacy challenge or point of friction. End with a question or CTA.

### 8.4 Tone Calibration for AI

When generating content for Corum:
- **Does this sound like generic SaaS copy?** Rewrite with pharmacy-specific language.
- **Is this too abstract?** Rewrite with clear operational outcomes.
- **Does this sound like a feature list without a business result?** Add the outcome.
- **Would a pharmacy owner, pharmacist, or front-of-shop team recognise this immediately?** If not, simplify.
- **Am I writing to impress, or to help?** Prioritise clarity over jargon.
